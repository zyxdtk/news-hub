Abstract
Language model loss follows remarkably regular scaling laws over model and data size, yet it remains unclear why the aggregate loss should exhibit a power-law form.
Existing explanations often attribute this regularity to a heavy-tailed spectrum of pattern difficulty in natural language, but this view has not been directly validated at token-level granularity in large-scale real-data training.
We present a token-level framework that decomposes scaling laws into localized learning events of individual contextualized tokens.
By fitting token loss trajectories with sigmoids, we show that token learning is concentrated in localized transitions, giving rise to a learning-time spectrum that dominates the scaling-law shape.

Limitations and Future Work.
This work makes a substantial step toward explaining the origin of neural scaling laws by resolving aggregate power-law behavior into token-level learning events and their learning-time spectrum, thereby prov

为宏观损失为何遵循如此规律的形式提供了更深入的机制性理解。尽管取得了这些进展，未来工作仍有几个重要的方向有待探索。首先，当前的分析计算成本高昂，因为它需要在多个规模轴上进行训练配方扫描、密集检查点保存以及词元级轨迹拟合，这目前限制了对更大实验网格和更多模型家族的更广泛覆盖。其次，我们目前的数据重塑实验是在样本级而非词元级进行的，因为自回归的下一词元预测在前向传播过程中将每个目标词元与其前置上下文耦合在一起。因此，目前的干预措施应被视为对测得的学习时间信号具有可操作性的初步证明，而更细粒度的词元级重塑和更完整的课程式调度仍是未来工作的重要方向。
