Retrieval answers “what did the user say earlier?” at query time. Consolidation asks which experiences should leave a persistent parametric imprint on how the agent writes and responds, rather than remain lookup-only facts. We operationalize this question through measurable parametric signatures (§4, §4.10–§4.11), not claims about cognitive identity. The 2024-2026 landscape of LLM-memory systems has produced excellent retrieval architectures — Mem0, Zep, Letta/MemGPT (Packer et al. , 2023 ) , HippoRAG (Gutiérrez et al. , 2024 ) , LIGHT (Tavakoli et al. , 2025 ) , EmergenceMem and Mastra OM — that achieve 90 90 - 99 % 99\% on verbatim- recall benchmarks such as LongMemEval (Wu et al. , 2024 ) and LoCoMo (Maharana et a

l. , 2024 ) . These systems work, and we do not propose to replace them. They are also, by construction, retrieval systems: they store and look up; they do not consolidate into the model’s parameters. This paper takes up the orthogonal question: what does a selective parametric consolidation mechanism look like, and how does it behave across LLM scales? Specifically, we investigate whether a parameter-efficient LLM can exhibit measurable signatures consistent with selective persona consolidation — deciding which fragments of stream content are significant enough to admit to a slow parametric store — in a way that admits falsifiable mechanistic measurements and derivable cross-scale rules. We position our work in the lineage of mechanistic interpretability for memory (ROME (Meng et al. , 2022 ) ; the linear-representation hypothesis (Park et al. , 2024 ) ; superposition (Elhage et al. , 2022 ) ) rather than as a competitor to retrieval-augmented systems on benchmarks they were designed for; we are explicit in §6.3 about retrieval and EVAF operating on different timescales (query-time vs. cumulative persona-formation) and being architecturally complementary, not zero-sum. Why “persona consolidation” and not just “memory”? The classical complementary- learning-systems (CLS) framework (McClelland et al. , 1995 ; Kumaran et al. , 2016 ) posits that a hippocampal fast store rapidly binds arbitrary episodes while a neocortical slow store consolidates only the subset that interacts w

ith prior knowledge and emotional salience. In humans and other animals, this selective consolidation is what produces personality and identity — the cumulative shape of the cortical store after a lifetime of experiences — not what produces fact lookup (Eichenbaum, 2017 ; Schapiro et al. , 2017 ) . The biological filter is a dual gate: novel content (high surprise) that is also emotionally tagged (high valence) is what shapes the slow store. EVAF instantiates this dual-gate selectivity in an LLM at modern scale; the §4 mechanistic signatures are consistent with selective parametric consolidation and a measurable persona-imprint signature — not a proof of identity formation or query-time recall superiority. The closest prior LLM-side work that operationalises a CLS-like pattern is Larimar (Das et al. , 2024 ) (external content-addressable episodic memory, parameter-free at write), FastSlow networks (Mujika et al. , 2017 ) , OML (Javed and White, 2019 ) , and FearNet (Kemker and Kanan, 2018 ) ; none of these contact LLM hidden-state geometry at modern scale, none implement a dual valence- × \times -surprise gate, and none provide the mechanistic-evidence protocols that this paper centres. Section 7 lists eleven honest limitations. 2 Background and Related Work 2.1 Complementary Learning Systems (CLS) and dual-store memory The complementary learning systems hypothesis (McClelland et al. , 1995 ) posits that the brain maintains two qualitatively distinct memory systems — a fast-binding hippocampal store and a slow-consolidating neocortical store — that exchange information through replay during off-task periods. Subsequent work refined the timing (Kumaran et al. , 2016 ; Schapiro et al. , 2017 ) and identified gating signals (novelty / reward) that determine which hippocampal episodes get consolidated. EVAF is inspired by CLS but is not a cognitive model : we do not claim to map our Prefix-state attractor to hippocampus or our LoRA-augmented weights to cortex. We use the dual-store architectural pattern as a design constraint and reserve neuroscience interpretation for §6 (Discussion). Three recent ML systems share this architectural pattern. FearNet (Kemker and Kanan, 2018 ) trains a dual-net for incremental classification with generative replay. OML (Javed and White, 2019 ) meta-learns representations to minimise forgetting. FastSlow networks (Mujika et al. , 2017 ) alternate fast-RNN inner steps with slow-weight consolidation. None of these contact LLM-scale hidden-state geome
