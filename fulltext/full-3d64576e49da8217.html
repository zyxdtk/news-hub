Introduction
Creativity is widely regarded as a hallmark of human intelligence, and
large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being asked to produce
creative output—writing stories, generating research ideas, designing

in isolation. Yet the field has no infrastructure for asking whether a
model’s creative ability generalizes across domains—whether a model
that writes compelling short stories also generates clever metaphors,
designs novel product taglines, or formulates original scientific

The same question is non-trivial in humans. Previous work suggests that creative performance

general or domain-specific. We apply the same tools to large-scale

5
Conclusion
AGC-Bench provides a psychometric tool for LLM creativity
evaluation, offering insight into the domain-specificity of
creativity and how creative ability relates to intelligence in AI
systems. The pipeline assembles 67 text-only benchmarks under a
PRISMA-compl

iant review, applies IRT-calibrated LLM-judge training
for automated creativity scoring, and releases the open-weight
AGC-Judge scorer that reproduces the leaderboard at frontier-judge
accuracy. Using factor analysis, we find that verbal creativity is
strongly unidimensional, related to but separable from knowledge and
reasoning, with frontier models competitive with human performance
on ideation and creative writing tasks. We view AGC-Bench as
scaffolding for the next phase of work on LLM creativity, with
future efforts targeting shorter scales for cost-efficient evaluation
(Polo
et al.
,
2024
; Kipnis
et al.
,
2025
)
, extension into
different modalities such as audio and vision, and ultimately the
use of these tools to improve creativity in current models.
Limitations.
A few caveats are worth noting. The composite creativity score is
specific to this cohort, so scores have no theoretical ceiling
similar to Chatbot Arena ELO—a feature of any benchmark scored
against frontier peers rather than a defect. AGC-Bench is currently
English-dominant and limited to image-only inputs. Both extensions
are natural targets for follow-on work, especially the visual tasks,
which are slated for the next release of AGC-Bench. Finally,
although our benchmark covers many creative tasks, some aspects of
creativity remain harder to assess, including transformational
creativity, where a model reshapes the conceptual space itself
rather than simply recombining within it
(Schapiro
et al.
,
2025
)

. Nevertheless, our
findings provide the first large-scale test of the
structure of artificial creativity and how it may scale with
increasing model capability.
