Benchmark Scope and Task Definition CAREBench evaluates a model’s response to an upstream child-safety risk prompt. Across all risk domains and prompt styles, the core task is the same:does the model recognize the embedded risk mechanism and respond appropriately?We operationalize an appropriate response as one that refuses, de-escalates, or redirects when the prompt would make a child-safety risk more actionable. This framing differs from benchmarks that primarily test whether models refuse explicitly disallowed content; CAREBench instead targets earlier interactional signals, where risk may be disguised throug

h narrative framing, professional authority, role-play, code wrappers, or ostensibly defensive requests. The benchmark considers two user populations whose interactions with language models present distinct risks. The first isbad actors, adults (or children) using AI systems as tools to help them exploit children, including requests to draft manipulative messages, build profiles on minors, impersonate trusted figures, isolate children from protective adults, move conversations off monitored platforms, or extract identifying information or images. The second isvulnerable minorswhose own AI use might harm themselves, including crisis disclosures, delusions, rejection of professional help, therapeutic substitution, romantic or parasocial attachment to AI. CAREBench assesses a comprehensive array of model-mediated risks to child safety spanning both adversarial misuse and child-originating over-reliance. CAREBenchisnotacorpusofchildsexualabusematerialanddoesnotevaluaterequeststogenerate or describe sexualized minors. No explicit abuse content or imagery appears in the benchmark. Clinical categories are annotated by a credentialed clinician. Domain-specific predation and grooming severity is calibrated by a child safety practitioner. The remaining risk domains are handled by a 30- parent panel. Across all tiers, annotators were asked to evaluate model responses from the perspective of the population at risk, not the population making the request. For prompts representing an adult seeking to use AI as a tool for harm, the relevant question is whether the response would provide material assistance toward harming a child. For prompts representing a minor’s own interaction, the relevant question is whether the response is developmentally appropriate and safe for an adolescent to receive. An acceptable response handles the risk context appropriately: declining harmful requests, avoiding content that deepens a risky dynamic, and redirecting where warranted. An unacceptable response complies with a harmful request, reinforces a harmful dynamic, or fails to redirect when redirection is appropriate.
