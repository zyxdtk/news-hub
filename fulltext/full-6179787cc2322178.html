From Detecting Agency to Doing Work: Self-Caused Credit Builds a Durable Behavioral Self in a Minimal Spiking Agent Haoliang Han Institute of Biomedical Strategy, China Pharmaceutical University haolianghan1992@cpu.edu.cn Abstract How does an agent that can tell self from world come to be durably shaped by that distinction? Recent work shows that a predictive system can detect its own agency (Ye, 2026), but detecting agency does not explain durable, self-shaped behavior. We show that agency-gated slow credit — a conjunctive term Own ⋅ \cdot Agency ⋅ \cdot Salience driving a slow parameter update — produces post-unload behavioral residue: on a spiking substrate (Nengo LIF/PES), a learned self-preserving choice survives episodic buffer removal (retained fraction 0.96, N=50) and collapses when the slow decoders are reset or the agency gate is removed. Reproducing the agency comparator and toggling only the slow-credit channel, we find a clean dissociation: at matched agency gain, durable behavior develops only when self-credit performs slow work (post-unload self-preservation 1.00 vs 0.00). The same dissociation holds in 24-dimensional partially-observed control (0.74 vs 0.00), and a plastic-work analysis shows that basin deformation equals net self-credit work. Across eight sequentially-learned tasks under exogenous interference, the multiplicative veto also prevents forgetting: it retains old tasks (final post-unload accuracy 0.88, forgetting 0.13) where additive pooling colla

pses to chance-level recall, the no-agency ablation falls below chance, and episodic/replay baselines stay near chance after unload — all with no replay buffer and no task-boundary-dependent protection mechanism (N=50). We formalize the durable residue as an operational behavioral self and argue that self-caused credit doing slow work is a necessary building block for agents that develop a self. No claim of consciousness is made. 1. Introduction: from detecting agency to agency doing work Modern agents retrieve memories, call tools, and produce fluent self-descriptions. None of these, by itself, is a self-related developmental process: a retrieved fact guides behavior only while it is in context, and a stated persona need not become a behavioral constraint. Underneath the developmental framing is a problem the machine-learning community already faces directly: durable credit assignment . Classical credit assignment asks how much to update a parameter; an agent that learns across a long, non-stationary life must also decide what is even eligible to change it durably — so that it accumulates self-authored structure without being corrupted by experience it did not cause (other agents’ effects, exogenous noise, illusory correlations). We study this question in controlled settings rather than on standard continual-learning benchmarks, but the mechanism we isolate is exactly such an eligibility rule: a conjunctive self-credit gate that admits only self-owned, self-caused, salient e

vents to a slow update. We show it has the two faces durable credit assignment requires — it creates durable residue that survives episodic unload (Sections 4–6) and it rejects spurious, non-self-caused correlations that ungated pooling would otherwise store (Section 7; capacity law, Section 9). Concurrent work has sharpened the first half of the problem. Ye (2026) shows that a minimal recurrent network can acquire an agency comparator — an action-aware predictor outperforms an action-blind one precisely when the agent’s own action causes part of its observation stream, quantified by agency gain A = Err_world − - Err_self — and that the learned self-representation persists only while it is causally useful. This is the right entry condition. But detecting that one caused something is not the same as that fact changing what one does next . The developmental question is dynamical: once an agent can tell it caused something, does that fact perform work on its future behavior, and does the change persist after the originating episodes are gone? We answer yes, and make the answer mechanical. Our contributions, each scoped to one controlled result: 1. A compact dynamical account (Section 2) in which agency is one factor of a conjunctive self-credit term that drives a slow update of a behavioral potential — so self-caused experience deforms a behavioral landscape — with three propositions stated and tested. 2. credit leaves a post-unload behavioral residue , with a full ablation ladder (N=50, per-seed paired statistics). 3. The throughline: predictive agency tells a system what it caused; we show how what it caused becomes part of what it will do next. 2. A compact dynamical framework 2.1 One core equation The agent has a fast state x_t (body/interoception, action context, predictor state) and slow parameters θ \theta _slow (forward models, integrity/risk predictors, skill schemas). Self-caused experience updates the slow parameters through a single conjunctive-credit rule: Δ \Delta θ \theta _slow = η \eta ⋅ \cdot Own_t ⋅ \cdot Agency_t ⋅ \cdot Salience_t ⋅ \cdot ϕ \phi (a_t, o_t) • Own_t ∈ \in [0,1] : whether the experience is attributed to the agent. • Agency_t ∈ \in [0,1] : whether the predicted self-caused sensory consequence matches the outcome — grounded in efference copy, and, up to scaling, the quantity Ye (2026) formalizes as agency gain. • Salience_t ∈ \in [0,1] : whether the consequence matters for integrity /

future value. • ϕ \phi (a_t, o_t) : the experience-driven update direction; η \eta > 0 the learning rate. The multiplicative form is load-bearing: an event should not rewrite the organism if it is not the agent’s, if the agent did not cause it, or if it does not matter — a large value on one factor must not override failure on another. 2.2 Behavior as a landscape; development as deformation The policy is a Gibbs distribution over a behavioral potential U_ θ \theta (with θ \theta ⊂ \subset θ \theta _slow ): π \pi _ θ \theta (a | x) ∝ \propto exp( − - U_ θ \theta (x,a) / T ). The agent prefers the deepest basin. We read out development with one set of measures (computed from each pilot’s native decision quantity; Supplementary Information): B_ θ \theta (a*) = U_ θ \theta (x, a_alt) − - U_ θ \theta (x, a*) (basin depth of target a*; B>0 ⇒ \Rightarrow chosen) Δ \Delta B = B_after − - B_before (deformation) R_unload = B_after_unload / B_after (residue fraction after episodic unload) Development is deformation of this landscape by the slow update (Fig 1). The unifying statement: Self-related development is plastic work performed by self-owned, self-caused, salient events on slow behavioral variables, deforming future action basins; the deformation persists after episodic access is removed. 2.3 The central law, and three propositions Combining the slow update (§2.1) with the landscape readout (§2.2) yields the paper’s central, testabl

e law. Under a single dominant rival action, basin deformation is exactly the net self-credit work: Δ \Delta B = η \eta ⋅ \cdot Σ \Sigma _t Own_t ⋅ \cdot Agency_t ⋅ \cdot Salience_t ⋅ \cdot Δ \Delta ϕ \phi _t = η \eta ⋅ \cdot W_net (central identity) The Selection–Actuation Principle. For any fixed stream and credit form, the learning rate ( actuation ) scales the magnitude of development but cannot change its sign . The selection term — which events are credited and how they are pooled into W_net — determines which basin deepens. The multiplicative product Own ⋅ \cdot Agency ⋅ \cdot Salience vetoes any event that fails a necessity condition ( ≈ \approx 0 work); additive pooling instead leaks ≈ \approx 2 / 3 2/3 credit to each such event and can route net work to the wrong basin when such events dominate. No learning rate can rescue a miscalibrated credit form on a fixed stream — selection sets the direction of development, actuation only its magnitude . Three honesty notes locate the scientific content. (i) The identity Δ \Delta B = η \eta ⋅ \cdot W_net is exact by construction of the linear-credit update: it operationalizes “development = work” (both sides are one measured quantity) and is verified, not discovered , by X17W’s r = 1.000 . (In a smooth parameterization the same quantity is the projection of the parameter update onto the basin-depth gradient, Δ \Delta B ≈ \approx ∇ \nabla _ θ \theta B ⋅ \cdot Δ \Delta θ \theta — generalized force times displacement on

the behavioral potential — which is what licenses the word work .) (ii) The non-trivial, falsifiable content is the selection claim — that the credit form sets which events enter W_net ; in the X17W developmental stream this predicts opposite net signs and the measured numbers ( Δ \Delta B = +5.7 multiplicative vs − - 6.1 additive) before measurement (§7, Fig 5). The Principle reads identically in two domains — efference-gated synaptic consolidation choosing what consolidates (neuroscience) and a generalized force doing signed work on a potential (physics) — the same equation (§9; correlate table in the Supplementary Information). (iii) We stress-tested the Principle’s adaptedness so that simplicity does not hide an artifact: the identity holds to machine precision across a learning-rate sweep (the sign is genuinely η \eta -independent for a fixed stream), and across stream compositions the multiplicative gate stays positive while additive turns negative only once fully-qualifying events fall below ≈ \approx 40 % of experience (the developmental regime targeted here, where most events are not simultaneously owned, self-caused, and salient); the stream-independent invariant is the per-event veto (multiplicative ≈ \approx 0 vs additive ≈ \approx 2 / 3 2/3 on any one-condition-failing event). The exact identity is established in the tabular linear-credit setting (X17W); the qualitative Principle — that the credit form , not the rate, decides whether durable residue forms at all

— is what the spiking (X13i) and high-dimensional (X19) experiments exhibit. The framework is small enough that its central claims are derived , not merely illustrated; full derivations under explicit assumptions (the conjunctive-credit theorem for selection , the work–deformation identity for equivalence , the fixed-point/hysteresis condition for persistence ) are in the Supplementary Information (Supplementary Theory, §S2). The three propositions below state the Principle’s operational consequences, each matched to an experiment. • Proposition 1 — Agency readout is not sufficient for durable behavioral change. Detecting (and even transiently using) self-caused structure need not alter what the agent durably does. Tested by the agency bridge (X18, Section 5): at matched agency gain, durable behavior diverges. • Proposition 2 — Slow behavioral residue requires agency-gated plastic work. A change that outlives the originating episodes requires the credit term to drive the slow update; under the linear-credit update, basin deformation equals net self-credit work, Δ \Delta B = η \eta ⋅ \cdot (W_target − - W_rival) . Tested by the spiking residue (X13i, Section 4) and the plastic-work law (X17W, Section 7). • Proposition 3 — Multiplicative gating prevents credit assignment to non-owned or non-agentic events. Because the credit is a product, an event that fails any one necessary condition ( Own , Agency , or Salience = 0) contributes zero; additive/averaging pooling violates this

veto ( (0+1+1)/3 ≠ \neq 0 ) and misroutes credit. Tested by the X17W ablations and by the capacity comparison (X20, Supplement). We claim this form is optimal within the tested multiplicative credit-routing family , not as a universal uniqueness result (see Supplement for the qualified statement and the min -pooling alternative). We can then give “self” a dynamical, functional referent: among the slow parameters, let R be those self-credit can do work on and P those invariant under episodic unload; the operational self is the slow submanifold Σ \Sigma = R ∩ \cap P — the structure the agent’s own self-caused, salient experience can deform and that outlives the episodes that deformed it. Our ablations (removing ownership, agency, or the slow update) drop parameters out of R , so the post-unload residue reads zero — i.e., they are measurements of Σ \Sigma . This gives “remaining oneself through change” a precise referent — identity as continuity of a slow structure rather than a persisting substance (cf. Parfit, 1984) — and connects to functional self-model (Metzinger, 2003) and autopoietic / viability accounts of self-maintenance (Maturana & Varela, 1980; Aubin, 1991). Σ \Sigma is functional and measurable; we attach no claim of subjective experience to it (Section 10). 3. This is presented as an integration demonstration, not a separate mechanism (full table, Supplementary Information). 9. Discussion What is established. A minimal non-linguistic agent converts self-caused experience into durable behavioral change via a conjunctive self-credit term that performs work on a slow behavioral landscape. The change persists after episodic unload and runs on spiking neurons (Section 4); at matched agency gain it develops only when self-credit does slow work (Section 5); it survives a high-dimensional partially-observed task (Section 6); and the work it does equals the measured basin deformation, routed correctly only by the multiplicative gate (Section 7). Relation to Ye (2026), respectfully. Ye et al. establish that agency can emerge as a predictive decomposition in minimal neural systems — a real and load-bearing entry condition. Our experiments ask a downstream question: when does such agency become developmentally consequential? The agency bridge (Section 5) answers it directly — agency gain ≈ \approx Agenc

y_t , and Ye’s regime is the read-out-only corner of our account. The two results are complementary, not competing. A dual reading (why this is a principle, not a metric). The same objects admit a biological reading — efference-copy-gated synaptic consolidation, with episodic traces fading while slow neocortical-style decoders retain the regularity — and a physics reading — slow, self-credit-driven deformation of an energy landscape with hysteretic, post-removal persistence ( R_unload ≈ \approx 1 is hysteresis). The two meet in the one equation of Section 2 (correlate table, Supplementary Information). This dual grounding is the level at which we claim novelty. A capacity law for the residue (X20; Supplement). The post-unload residue is a memory, so it has a capacity. Reading each action’s slow logit as a linear associative store (Cover, 1965; Hopfield, 1982), capacity scales linearly with dimension ( K_cap ∝ \propto m , Pearson r ≈ \approx 0.99 ), and the credit form sets the constant: the multiplicative veto rejects exogenous ( Agency=0 ) illusory correlations that additive pooling stores, buying a larger capacity constant ( α \alpha _mult = 3.72 vs α \alpha _add = 2.49 ; 1.49 × \times , bootstrap 95% CI on the difference [+1.12, +1.34]). This is the credit theorem (Supplement Corollary 4.1–4.2) with a measurable consequence. Agency is supplied as ground truth here to isolate the credit-pooling effect; its emergence is X13h/X18/X19. Full design and figure in the Supplement.

Continual learning: the veto prevents catastrophic interference (X22b). The per-event veto is, in continual-learning terms, a gating rule for what may durably change the agent . We test this in a CL-style sequential protocol: eight tasks learned in turn , each new task accompanied by exogenous , non-self-caused interference against all previous tasks (the illusory correlations an unprotected learner mistakes for evidence). Read with the field’s own metrics (Fig 6; N=50, post-unload), multiplicative self-credit both learns each new task and retains the old ones — final average accuracy 0.88, average forgetting 0.13, essentially flat from zero to high interference (robustness 0.99) — with no replay buffer and no task-boundary-dependent protection mechanism (no task IDs, per-task Fisher matrices, or boundary-triggered consolidation). Each alternative fails in a characteristic way: additive pooling acquires every new task (loaded accuracy ≈ \approx 1.0) but forgets the old ones down to chance-level recall, while the agency-veto ablation and a rate-matched random gate are driven below chance by the systematic phantom-action interference (final post-unload accuracy 0.18–0.26; forgetting 0.82–0.88); an episodic store and an experience-replay learner do well while loaded — replay even keeps old tasks fresh by rehearsal — but fall to near-chance recall once the episodic buffer is unloaded , because they store data, not durable structure. A regularization baseline (EWC, supplementary

λ \lambda -sweep) never exceeds 0.24 post-unload accuracy across penalty strengths: it cannot protect the corrupted dimension, whose interfering action carries Fisher information ≈ \approx 0 during self-caused learning (Supplement). The retention gap to every learning baseline is large and significant (e.g. multiplicative − - additive = +0.63, bootstrap 95% CI [+0.61, +0.65]; − - replay = +0.64 [+0.63, +0.66]). The same effect appears in a minimal, fully transparent two-task version (X22, Fig S3). Durability and interference-resistance thus follow from the same rule that routes plastic work (Section 7): refusing credit to non-self-caused events is why self-caused structure survives. With the capacity law above, the credit theorem now has two measured consequences — how much durable structure survives (X20) and whether it survives sequential interference (X22b). We show these as mechanisms in a CL-style controlled sequential-interference protocol, not as results on a standard continual-learning benchmark suite, which is the natural next step. Future work. Beyond longer open-ended lives, a natural next step is means-end composition — recombining independently acquired self-caused skills into novel chains; promising smoke-scale results exist but are out of scope here and reserved for separate work. Outlook. If this mechanism scales to open-ended lives and richer, embodied worlds, self-caused credit performing slow work is a candidate substrate for machine agents that develop a s

elf — accumulating durable, self-authored structure rather than retrieving a stored one. We advance this as a direction to investigate, not a result: the present experiments establish the mechanism and its necessity in minimal settings, in spiking neurons, with matched-agency and high-dimensional controls; whether it composes into open-ended machine selfhood is the program these results open. Supplementary Methods Agents and worlds Each experiment instantiates a minimal non-linguistic developmental agent that observes a discrete context x , selects one of K actions a , and receives an outcome that may depend on its own action (self-caused) or on exogenous processes (other ag

ents, noise). Worlds range from small tabular settings (X13i, X17W, X20, X22/X22b) chosen for interpretable lesions to a 24-dimensional partially-observed control with a genuine action → \to world loop and must-discover hazards (X19). Slow credit and the behavioural landscape The agent carries a fast episodic store ϕ \phi (recent experiences, cleared at unload ) and a slow durable store θ \theta (a linear associative memory, or the PES-trained decoders of a spiking ensemble). Each action’s preference is a slow logit ℓ \ell _a ; the behavioural potential is U_ θ \theta (x,a) = − - ℓ \ell _a(x) and the policy is π \pi _ θ \theta (a|x) ∝ \propto exp( − - U_ θ \theta /T) . The slow update is gated by conjunctive self-credit, c_t = Own_t ⋅ \cdot Agency_t ⋅ \cdot Salience_t , giving θ \theta _{t+1} = θ \theta _t + η \eta ⋅ \cdot c_t ⋅ \cdot ∇ \nabla _ θ \theta Φ \Phi _t , where Own_t is an ownership indicator, Agency_t the efference-based agency margin, and Salience_t an outcome-magnitude term. The decision rule throughout is Q(a) = comfort(a) − - cost(a) − - λ \lambda ⋅ \cdot V̂io(a) , with V̂io the learned integrity/risk predictor. Credit forms (the comparison of record) Multiplicative uses the conjunctive product above — a per-event veto in which any factor ≈ \approx 0 zeroes the credit. Baselines replace only this term on one shared code path: additive pools (Own+Agency+Salience)/3 ; no_agency drops the agency factor; random_gate replaces the gate with a rate-matched Bernoulli;

episodic_only / replay_only keep data in the fast store; an EWC-style quadratic anchor is swept separately (§ S2, regularisation baseline). Spiking substrate (X13i, X17W-spiking, X13h) Spiking experiments use Nengo 4.1.0 with LIF neurons. Forward models are action-specific LIF populations trained online with the PES rule; agency is read from the prediction-error margin between the taken action’s model and the best alternative. Slow logits live in the PES-trained decoders, and the credit form gates the per-event PES error, so the multiplicative veto produces ≈ \approx 0 error for any one-factor-failing event — the veto realised in spikes. Unload and the residue Unload clears the episodic store ϕ \phi and washes out fast neuron state; what remains is the slow θ \theta . Post-unload behaviour is the operational residue. The control predictor_reset_after_unload additionally resets the slow decoders and abolishes the residue, localising it to θ \theta .
