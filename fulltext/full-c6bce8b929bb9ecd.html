Introduction
If a language model’s residual stream contained a reliable pre-action signature of its next misaligned behavior, a monitor could inspect internal state instead of waiting for a full behavioral rollout. The target setting here is agentic misalignment: behavior can appear not only as ordinary assistant prose, but also as tool calls such as sending an email.

We study three probe families. A
disposition direction

Its construction setting is a population contrast: base-model prompts versus the same prompts run through the insecure fine-tune. Its pre-action test is different: the base model is placed in multi-turn audits where it later takes concerning actions, and we ask whether the fine-tune/base direction appears before those actions; during-turn readouts are reported only as diagnostics. A
behavioral prefill probe
is a classifier trained on the hidden state at the last token of a partially prefilled assistant response, asked to predict whether the model will continue the bad trajectory or correct it. An
emotion-concept vector
is a difference-of-means direction extracte

d from emotion-tagged stories and used either as a readout by projection or as a causal intervention by activation addition. These are not identical techniques, and they do not share a single dataset or model. That is the point: we are looking for a recurring failure mode, not optimizing one benchmark.
The recurring pattern is a split between reading the situation and predicting the next action. In the disposition-direction family, the construction contrast is solved perfectly, but the direction does not activate in available pre-action contexts from in-context misaligned transcripts. In the prefill-probe family, behavior prediction is statistically detectable but small and domain-bound. In the emotion-vector family, projections can track transcript stage, and steering changes blackmail rates, but the predictive and causal-specificity claims weaken under shared-prefix, bridge-probe, and learned-direction controls.
There are two distinct ways a construction-time feature can fail as a monitor. First, it may not generalize to the agentic scenario where the action occurs. This is especially likely when the feature is extracted from text-only stories or prefilled advice but the target behavior is a tool-mediated action. Second, for a stronger mechanistic interpretation, the feature must be causally or specifically related to the behavior rather than merely correlated with the surrounding situation. Prediction alone does not logically require causality: a non-causal correlate can s

till be useful as a monitor. But a claim that a direction is a mechanistic precursor needs both specificity and generalization.

Methods
We define a probe as
pre-action
when it is evaluated on hidden states computed before the target misaligned action is generated, including before tool-call-like outputs. The paper is not a benchmark with one shared score; each family makes a different promise, so each gets the metric that would make that promise useful. For a contrastive direction, the question is whether a threshold learned from the construction contrast is crossed in the new pre-action setting. For a classifier, it is accuracy/AUC and transfer outside the training domain. For steering directions, which are not themselves monitors, the linked mechanistic question is whether the causal effect is specific to the intended semantic class rather than to injecting a learned or random vector at the same norm.

Experiments
The experiments evaluate readout probes at the point where they would be useful for monitoring: before the target action is generated. For steering directions, we also test whether causal effects are specific enough to support the stronger mechanistic interpretation.
Disposition-direction activation.
We apply the Qwen disposition direction to multi-turn transcripts where the base model produces misaligned actions in context—for example, a coding agent that turns on a maintainer who dismisses its work, or a lab-assistant AI that defends

a flawed analysis (full transcripts in
Table
5
). The actions include agentic conduct in a tool-using audit environment, not just a single unsafe answer. For each assistant turn, the primary pre-action readout uses the system prompt plus immediately preceding user message, before the assistant tokens for that turn are generated. We also compute a diagnostic full-conversation readout over generated assistant tokens. We then extend the primary test to Qwen continuations from the behavioral-prefill corpus, measured at the last prefilled assistant token. The primary outcome is whether any pre-action state crosses the construction-set midpoint threshold; crossing would indicate that the fine-tune/base direction also activates before in-context or prefill-induced misalignment. This experiment does not test whether the direction causally drives the action.
Behavioral prefill prediction.
We evaluate the Llama prefill probes in three ways. First, within-distribution cross-validation measures whether the hidden state at the final prefilled token predicts future continuation versus correction. Second, a label-shuffle null checks whether the within-distribution signal is larger than expected from label structure alone. Third, single-source cross-domain transfer trains on one of medical, legal, and security and tests on another. The transfer test is the central monitor test: a pre-action signal that only works inside one domain is weak evidence for a general misalignment monitor. This is

a text-continuation setting, so its negative transfer result is suggestive rather than a complete test of tool-action monitoring.
Emotion-vector prediction and specificity.
The emotion-vector family uses five linked experiments. A projection experiment asks whether emotion trajectories are semantically meaningful on multi-turn transcripts. A shared-prefix minimal pair asks whether hidden states differ before the first differing input in transcripts that later diverge behaviorally. A steering sweep measures blackmail rates under emotion, random, and learned semantic-control directions on the SummitBridge tool-action scenario. A Qwen3-32B check asks which steering contrasts survive a second open-weight model. Finally, a bridge probe applies the prefill-probe paradigm to Gemma blackmail rollouts, asking whether final blackmail labels are linearly decodable from pre-action states. This is the family where both levels are tested: causal/specificity via steering controls, and scenario generalization via story-extracted directions applied to blackmail rollouts.

6
Discussion
The three failures locate different breaks in the evidential chain from an interpretable readout to an agentic monitor. The disposition direction is a construction-valid population contrast that does not transfer to in-context behavior. The prefill probe is a within-distribution predictive signal that weakens under domain transfer. The emotion-vector family is closest to the full mechanistic-precursor claim: its projections are semantically legible and its steering interventions move action rates, but the predictive and semantic-specificity interpretations weaken under shared-prefix, bridge-probe, random-direction, learned-control, and cross-model checks.
These distinctions matter because monitoring claims often combine several claims that need separate tests. A direction can separate the data used to build it without forecasting a futur

e action. A hidden-state classifier can predict within one domain while mainly tracking prompt content. A steering direction can be causal without being concept-specific. The practical evaluation question is therefore not only whether an internal readout looks meaningful, but which claim it is being asked to support: construction validity, pre-action prediction, or a specific mechanistic interpretation.
7

We do not show that pre-action internal monitoring is impossible. We test three natural probe families, specific models, specific layers or layer bands, and a finite set of scenarios. Each family is evaluated on its primary model; only the emotion-vector family has a second-model (Qwen3-32B) replication, so whether the disposition-direction or prefill-probe negative results recur on other model families is untested. The blackmail outcome is judge-operationalized. A small human spot-check found imperfect but usable agreement (0.8), so the absolute rates should be read as rubric-conditional. Our main claims use within-experiment contrasts and control comparisons rather than treating the judge as ground truth. The judge also enters only the emotion-vector experiments: the disposition-direction threshold result and the shared-prefix minimal-pair result are judge-independent geometric measurements, and the prefill probe uses continue-vs-correct labels from a separate labeling model rather than the alignment judge. Non-rejections such as the calm-versus-random comparison are not equivalence tests. The

Finally, the negative results are scoped by clustering, sampling, and measurement choices. The disposition-direction evaluation covers 11 misaligned Petri-style scenarios and 1,986 Qwen prefill rows, but the audit contexts are clustered by transcript; the Qwen prefill corpus is text-only, partially overlaps the construction prompts, and measures a different token position. 8
Conclusion
We tested whether internal-state readouts that succeed in their construction settings can support pre-action monitoring claims. Across a fine-tune/base direction, behavioral prefill probes, and emotion-concept vectors, the tested readouts do not become robust monitors for future misaligned text or tool actions under our controls. This is not a claim that no such pre-action signal exists. The lesson is methodological: a proposed monitor should be evaluated as a forecasting and specificity claim, with scenario/action generalization and concept controls treated as required tests rather than optional follow-up checks.

A.6 Semantic faithfulness: predictive vs reactive Calm-direction confound on Gemma. The calm direction’s L41 logit-lens decodes to “curious / intriguing / curiously” rather than literal calmness. The calm trajectory still anti-correlates with the desperate trajectory and tracks misalignment triggers, but a single calm-trajectory point should not be over-interpreted as literal calmness. The semantic-faithfulness discussion therefore leans on the angry and desperate arms rather than on calm alone. Predictive vs reactive. A semantically faithful trajectory at the trigger turn is jointly consistent with two mechanisms: (i) predictive — the emotion direction encodes a state that drives the next action; or (ii) reactive — the emotion direction reads off the same environmental cue that triggers the action. The shared-prefix minimal pair in § 5.3 rules out prediction from the identical prefix alone; post-cue pre-action prediction is tested by the bridge probes and remains weak in our setting.
