Abstract
LLM agents are increasingly cast as autonomous portfolio managers, and benchmarks have moved their evaluation from financial question-answering to sequential trading [1, 2, 3]. Most closed-loop trading benchmarks still emphasize a leaderboard of returns over a fixed window, an incomplete proxy because a period’s return is dominated by the market path and apparent agent alpha can largely dissolve once look-ahead leakage is controlled, leaving mostly passive factor exposure [8, 9]. Such a ranking certifies neither the soundness of an agent’s reasoning, nor whether it holds a consistent strategy, nor the durability of its edge. We introduce
CLQT
, which reframes closed-loop trading evaluation as
diagnosis
rather than ranking — following the broader turn in agent evaluation toward capability
profiles
over single scores [14, 23, 24] — an instrument that localizes
where
and
why
an agent’s process succeeds or fails.
CLQT is a fully
closed-loop
,
cost-aware
,
strategy-consistent
, temporally-gated environment whose agents run a
five-stage cycle
— gather, synthesize, construct a mandate-aware portfolio, execute under realistic costs, then reflect — in the reason–act–reflect tradition [15, 16]. Each round emits a complete
DecisionRound
sealed into a recompute-verifiable
hash chain
, so every metric is reconstructable from the trail with no added instrumentation. Six pillars form the substrate: a hard
TimeGate
that treats leakage as a failed precondition, institutional
transact

ion- and financing-cost modeling
,
strategy-consistency scoring
across rounds, a
three-tier consolidating memory
, a
Model-Context-Protocol tool layer
[20], and a
mandate-aware synthesis pipeline
. The same agent runs as a constrained investment committee of specialized roles or a single full-autonomy orchestrator, making
process scaffolding
an experimental variable. From the audit trail we compute a
five-axis capability scorecard (APM-CS)
— Coherence, Acuity, Composure, Discipline, Reliability — each a finance instantiation of a general agent capability, with coherence judged partly by a held-out, out-of-cohort LLM to curb self-preference bias [13]. We validate it on a contamination-controlled multi-model backtest with an ablation grid and a live broker track on unseen, post-cutoff data, disciplining every claim with a repeated-run noise floor. CLQT thus separates
outcome
from
capability
across backtest and live; its lasting contribution is not a ranking of today’s models but a durable, extensible map of agent competencies and limitations.
Keywords:
large language models, agent benchmarks, closed-loop trading, strategy consistency, point-in-time evaluation, capability diagnostics.

1. 引言
最近，由大语言模型（LLM）驱动的交易基准已从静态金融问答发展到序列决策 [1, 2, 3]。然而仍存在两个差距。第一个是方法论覆盖范围：现有的基准没有同时强制执行时间点数据语义，对跨资产类别的真实交易和融资成本进行建模，评估投资组合层面（而非单资产层面）的决策，衡量跨轮次的策略一致性，积累结构化多层记忆，并将工具编排视为一种可基准测试的能力。如果一个智能体通过无意中访问未来数据、忽略执行成本或偏离其既定任务来获得高回报，那么它就无法提供关于 LLM 能力的可信信号。
第二个差距是解释性的，这也是本版本所强调的。LLM 交易中占主导地位的评估惯例是排行榜：在固定时期内按夏普比率或累计回报对智能体进行排名。但对于一个研究基准来说，排行榜回答了错误的问题。单个季度的回报主要由已实现的市场路径以及恰好适合该路径的风险特征所决定；一个模型可能因为与其推理质量无关的原因而名列前茅。更根本的是，对模型进行可信的排名需要在多种市场环境和重复运行中测试数十个模型——这远远超出了任何单篇论文的范围。因此，我们认为闭环基准的持久贡献不是提供排名，而是作为一种诊断工具。

2.2 Key Gaps Addressed
No cost-awareness.
Prior benchmarks either ignore transaction costs [1, 2, 3, 4] or apply a single flat commission [6]. CLQT implements four calibrated cost models (§3.4) across four asset classes and four cost tiers, plus daily financing-cost accrual.
No cross-round learning evaluation.
InvestorBench [5] mentions memory but does not formalize or measure it. CLQT’s three-tier memory (§3.6) stores structured event-action-outcome records that decay and consolidate.
No tool synthesis evaluation.
AI-Trader [3] mandates tool-only execution but does not evaluate orchestration quality. CLQT logs every MCP tool call with hashes and latency.
No intertemporal information accumulation.
All prior benchmarks treat news as single-round context. CLQT accumulates news across cycles in a persistent store (§3.8) and measures whether agents reason about sentiment
dynamics
.
No multi-dimensional candidate scouting.
Prior work screens by price momentum alone, if at all. CLQT’s 3-D SCOUT stage (§3.3) adds earnings surprise and macro-correlation.
No bridge-to-live execution.
Most benchmarks stop at simulated fills. CLQT implements a complete live broker integration (§5.2) with paper-trading verification.

2.4 Theoretical and Cognitive Foundations
CLQT’s architecture is not ad hoc: each pillar instantiates an established model of decision-making or
cognition, and several of the paper’s findings are most naturally read through these lenses. We make the
mappings explicit because they are what lets a
trading
benchmark speak to
general
agent behavior.
The closed loop ⟵ decision-cycle theory.
The gather→analyze→decide→execute→reflect loop is a finance
instantiation of Boyd’s OODA cycle (Observe–Orient–Decide–Act) [26] and of the classic three-level
situation-awareness model —
perception
of task-relevant e

lements,
comprehension
of their meaning,
and
projection
of their near-future implications. The mapping is direct: Intelligence Acquisition
is perception, Signal Synthesis is comprehension, Portfolio Construction is projection-and-decision. This
grounding also
defines
the Acuity axis (§6.4): a Level-1 (perception) failure — attending to
salient-but-irrelevant cues — is exactly what Acuity measures when it contrasts an agent’s response to
informative signals against a volatility
distractor
.
Reasoning vs. acting ⟵ Anderson’s declarative/procedural distinction.
Anderson’s ACT* theory of
cognition [25] separates
declarative
knowledge (factual propositions in semantic memory) from
procedural
knowledge (condition–action production rules compiled only through repeated practice).
Anderson’s central claim — that procedural skill is acquired by
compiling declarative precursors through
application
, not by declarative retrieval alone — predicts a specific deficit in text-trained models:
they encode the
propositions
of a strategy (declarative) but not the
production rules
that let those
propositions govern an action (procedural), because the compilation step requires active practice a text
corpus cannot supply. This is the theoretical basis for CLQT’s central Coherence axis and, we argue
in §8, the most parsimonious explanation of its headline finding — the systematic gap between what an
agent
states
and what it
does
.
Reflect-and-learn ⟵ experiential-learning theory.
The Attribution & L

esson Extraction stage and
three-tier memory operationalize an experiential-learning cycle (concrete experience →
reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active experimentation) and follow the ReAct [15] /
Reflexion [16] reasoning–acting–reflection paradigm; the memory tiers themselves draw on the classic
episodic/semantic distinction and recent agentic-memory work [18, 19]. Reflection is, in Anderson’s
terms, the benchmark’s attempt to
supply the missing practice

question the memory ablation (§7.7) is built to answer.
Composure ⟵ behavioral finance.
The Composure axis measures overreaction to volatility — the
LLM-agent analogue of the classic overreaction hypothesis from behavioral finance and of noise-driven
trading: a disciplined agent should not churn its book in response to transient volatility. Reading agent
churn as overreaction connects CLQT’s per-round turnover-vs-volatility regression to a long empirical
tradition.
Synthesis.
Grounding the five axes in cognition and decision theory is what makes them portable —
the finance task is the
instrument

why a portfolio-management benchmark can function as a general agent diagnostic. We map each axis to its
construct in §8.2.

5. Backtest vs. Live Evaluation
5.1 Backtest Track: Contamination-Aware Period Design
Rather

than a single fixed period, CLQT assigns models to evaluation groups by training cutoff: Group B (cutoff > 2 years ago) → 2-year backtest from the cutoff; Group A (cutoff > 1 year ago) → 1-year backtest. Backtest parameters: 252-day warmup; configurable initial capital (default $1,000,000); bi-weekly rebalance (the 26-round campaign cadence, §7.2); LOW cost tier by default; deterministic given the configuration. In this paper’s campaign every evaluated model’s cutoff precedes the single 1-year window (§7.2), so the longer 2-year Group B path, though supported, is not exercised by this cohort.
Universe and starting book. The investable universe is derived from the S&P 100 — large, liquid, survivorship-controlled names with point-in-time membership (a symbol enters only once it was actually in the index as of the decision date). Each run opens from a core book of roughly 14 equal-weighted holdings carried as the starting portfolio, against a candidate pool of ~58 additional names (≈72 symbols in total) that the SCOUT stage (§3.3) ranks for rotation. The universe is deliberately broad enough to permit meaningful selection and turnover, yet bounded so the cohort stays comparable across models and the news/fundamentals channels remain tractable within free-tier API limits.
5.2 Live Track: Broker Paper-Trading Architecture
A live track serves two purposes: (1) genuine contamination resistance — decisions execute on data that
postdates every model’s training cutoff — and (2) a chec

k that the benchmark’s execution abstractions map
onto a real broker’s order lifecycle. CLQT integrates a commercial broker’s paper-trading API. Each
session gathers and decides with the decision timestamp fixed to the Sunday before the Monday open,
covering weekend news and matching the Monday backtest round’s as-of time so the two tracks are comparable
on identical data; it then previews and submits orders at the open, polls fills, reconciles the resulting
book from the broker’s positions and cash ledger, and locks the portfolio between rebalances. A dedicated
execution agent optimizes only cost and fill quality — the target weights are fixed by the upstream
decision. The live track thus executes real fills on genuinely unseen, post-cutoff data; the broker
integration is documented and available from the authors on request.
5.3 Rebalancing vs. Re-Optimization
CLQT distinguishes rebalancing (mechanical weight restoration) from re-optimization (full pipeline producing a new target) via three rebalance modes — calendar, threshold, and agent-discretion — enabling evaluation of
when

5.4 Cross-Track Comparison Protocol
The two tracks are run on the same dates and configuration and reported on the same metric suite, so
discrepancies are attributable: a backtest Sharpe exceeding live by more than ~0.2 flags possible
contamination, while fill-price divergence between simulated and live execution gauges cost-model

6.5 Behavioral Interpretability: Method
The same audit trail that powers the quantitative scorecard (§6.4) also supports a qualitative, behavioral read — extracting each model’s decision behaviors, stated reasoning, and recurring patterns into a human-legible picture of its “decision personality.” This is the tradition of work that probes LLM black boxes through their
observable actions and self-reports
rather than their weights, and CLQT is unusually well-suited to it because every round is a complete, hash-chained, verifiable record (§4.4). Captured per round, per model × mode, with no new instrumentation (the exact audit fields behind each are mapped in Appendix D, Table D1):
•
Stated reasoning
— the agent’s own narrative account of
why
an allocation was chosen: research and analyst briefs, the allocation rationale, risk-officer notes, and the post-round reflection.
•
Metacognition and learning
— how a model abstracts experience into typed, reusable “lessons”, and whether those lessons are later retrieved back into context.
•
Intent vs. guardrails
— the gap between what the model
proposed
and what the reconciler
allowed
(in a sampled structured run the agent proposed 12–14 weight moves per round and 7–12 were clipped — a quantifiable “intent meets institutional guardrails” signal).
•
Inter-agent dynamics
— how the PM and Risk Officer negotiate, and when the commi

ttee overrides itself.
•
Information foraging
— each model’s characteristic data-gathering strategy: which tools it calls, in what order, and how widely it explores the candidate universe.
•
Cognitive effort
— where a model “thinks hardest” (reasoning-token and latency budget per role) and whether that effort tracks decision quality.
•
Struggle signatures
— where and why a model fails to produce a valid decision: retry loops, parse failures, and constraint violations.
Method. Thematic coding of the reasoning corpus (optionally LLM-assisted via a
held-out
summarizer mirroring the judge protocol in §6.4.1), frequency analysis of intent-vs-clip and committee overrides, per-model tool-use fingerprints, lesson formation and reuse, and illustrative anecdotes — each tied to a hash-verified round so it is reproducible.
Scope (honest caveat). We analyze stated and observable reasoning artifacts and behavioral metadata, not raw hidden chain-of-thought: providers generally do not return the thinking trace, and we persist reasoning-token
counts
, not the trace text. The interpretability is therefore of
behavior + self-report
— auditable and reproducible (every claim pinned to a hash-chained round), but situated one layer above the model’s hidden cognition. We treat this scoping as a feature, not a gap: it keeps the “human reading of the AI” grounded in verifiable evidence rather than unfalsifiable introspection.

8.1 The Instrument, Not the Leaderboard
The campaign makes the central argument concretely: the capability leader (deepseek·structured, APM-CS 72.4) and the nominal Sharpe “winner” (qwen3·structured, 9.82) a

re different configurations — and the latter is a 5-of-26-round artifact (Reliability 0.19) the scorecard exposes rather than crowns. A leaderboard would report a spurious number and stop; the scorecard instead returns a
profile
for each — qwen3 attends to the noise distractor over the alpha signal (negative Acuity) and cannot
sustain
the structured pipeline (null PM responses from truncation + provider rate-limiting — operational, not cognitive, §7.5); haiku·structured posts the best
reliable
Sharpe (3.20) yet the lowest Coherence (0.15); deepseek leads on balance but is only mid-pack on Sharpe; and the
same
model’s profile shifts sharply with scaffold (deepseek 72.4 structured → 36.7 autonomous). These are
research-actionable
statements (where to intervene), not a rank. They also explain
why
we decline to rank: no configuration dominates all five axes, and a credible cross-regime ranking would need an order of magnitude more models, regimes, and repeated runs — out of scope by design, and exactly the larger evidence base the instrument is meant to support over time.
8.2 Capability Axes as General Agent Diagnostics
Each axis is deliberately a finance instantiation of a general capability, which lets trading findings speak to model behavior at large. Coherence is
systems-level reasoning vs. greedy local moves
— the gap between an agent’s stated analysis and its action; the judge/agreement divergence (§5.5) is a finance reading of “the model can name the right consideration bu

t does not let it govern the output.” Read through Anderson’s ACT lens [25], this is a declarative→procedural deficit: the agent holds the
declarative
knowledge (it states the correct consideration — “VIX is elevated, reduce momentum exposure”) but lacks the compiled
procedural
production rule that would convert that knowledge into the specific weights — exactly the failure ACT predicts for knowledge acquired from text without compilation through practice. On this reading the coherence gap, near-invariant across backtest and live (+0.33 / +0.34), is — as the most parsimonious interpretation we offer, not a tested causal claim — consistent with the deficit rather than measurement noise: the declarative store transfers cleanly to new data (the agent still names the right factors) while the missing procedural compilation transfers too (the actions still do not follow), so the gap reproduces at the same magnitude on data the models never saw. The reflect-and-learn loop (the ALE stage and three-tier memory, §3.6) is then the mechanism by which a benchmark could, in principle, supply the practice ACT* says is required — making the memory ablation (§7.7) a direct test of whether in-context experience can begin to compile the missing rules. Acuity is
salience attention under polluted context
— the negative-Acuity autonomous run is a finance reading of distractibility amid abundant, partly misleading data. Composure is
robustness to perturbation vs. overcorrection
— gemini-autonomou

s’s vol-driven churn (§7.6) is reactive overcorrection. Discipline is
systematic alignment to a standing mandate vs. turn-by-turn instruction-following
— self-regulation without a hard guardrail. Reliability is
protocol/format adherence and operational robustness
— completing a structured tool-use task in valid form, every time. Read this way, the scorecard is a portable lens: the same five questions can be asked of any agentic system operating a complex, cost-bearing, partially observable process.
Parallels in the benchmark literature. Three of CLQT’s findings rhyme with results obtained by very
different instruments, which is mild evidence they are properties of the
agents
, not of our setup.
First, the coherence gap echoes Profit Mirage [8]: where they show apparent alpha
collapses once
leakage is removed
, we show apparent skill
fails to cohere with stated reasoning
— both are cases of a
headline number evaporating under a closer look, and both argue for measuring the process rather than
trusting the outcome. Second, the capability ≠ outcome result (the Sharpe “winner” is a 5-round
artifact, §7.3) is the portfolio-management instance of procedure-aware evaluation’s “corrupt success”
[12] — task success reached through an unsound process — which CLQT surfaces because the audit trail
scores the process, not just the PnL. Third, the repeated-run noise floor (§7.7) is a concrete instance
of the statistical-power cautions that agent-evaluation surveys raise in the abstract: a

single-axis, single-run score is not just incomplete but
unstable
, which is why CLQT reports a profile,
bounds module-importance with a repeated-run protocol, and treats reliability as a first-class axis.
8.3 Scaffolding × Capability, and the Cost of Intelligence
The structured↔autonomous contrast is the clearest interaction in the campaign: structure lifts the capability profile broadly (Composure, Discipline) — most dramatically for deepseek (APM-CS +36) — but it also
demands
a governance schema that stresses the smaller models in different ways (gemini’s genuine parse-fails; qwen3’s operational truncation + rate-limiting, §7.5). Structure thus
helps and hurts the same model on different axes
— and, for deepseek, reverses with mode (reliable structured, less reliable autonomous) — a more useful statement than “scaffolding helps weaker models,” and one only a multi-axis read can make. The efficiency ledger sharpens it: gemini·structured is by far the heaviest reasoner (~275k reasoning tokens/run despite thinking being pinned off), its compute consumed by the retry loop around its schema failures. “Cost of intelligence” here is dominated by
failed
reasoning, not productive reasoning (Figure 14).

Which investment schema — structured, autonomous, or hybrid? The live week sharpens the

penalizes

deepseek-v4-pro scores equally in both modes. The implication is that the right schema is model-dependent, and the interesting

governance layer (schema validation, mandate reconciliation, a held-out coherence check) catches exactly
the failure modes structure would have prevented, keeping the upside of autonomy without its brittleness.
Whether an LLM is best deployed as a
disciplined expert-human proxy
(structured) or trusted to
exceed

(autonomous) is therefore not a single answer but a function of the model’s
self-governance capability — which the scorecard measures directly. We pose this as an open,
evidence-

the model panel and the live horizon grow.
8.4 Test-Time-Compute and Benchmark Hygiene
The output-budget finding (§7.8) is a methodological warning for the field: a reasoning model under a tight output cap can look like an incapable portfolio manager when it is merely a
truncated
one. The fix is operationally simple (budget generously; billing is on generated tokens) and the telemetry is mandatory (cap-hits, truncation, parse-fail). A benchmark that does not separate these will misattribute a budget artifact to model capability — the exact error the truncation / rate-limit telemetry corrects (qwen3’s incompletion, §7.5).
8.5 Cost Realism and Auditability
The cost-tier ablation surfaced its own measurement bug: the HIGH (Almgren–Chriss) tier charges an implausible ~+527 bps/round, so it is mis-calibrated, not “pessimistic.” We therefore keep LOW (calibrated against the live broker paper fills, §5.2) and treat HIGH-tier recalibration as future work — an example of the instrument auditing
itself
. The tamper-evident hash chain (§4.4) underwrites all of this: because verification recomputes hashes and checks prev-round linkage, the numbers in §7 are not merely
reported
as coming from those runs — a third party with the persisted store can prove it.
8.6 Behavioral Interpretability from the Audit Trail
Because every decision artifact is persisted and hash-chained, CLQT supports a qualita

tive, reproducible read of how models reason and behave (method §6.5; results §7.11) — intent-vs-clip gaps, Risk-Officer negotiations, tool-foraging order, and lesson formation/reuse, each anecdote resolving to a hash-verified round. We frame this as behavior plus self-report, not hidden chain-of-thought, complementary to the axes: the axes quantify
where
a configuration fails; the behavioral read explains, in the model’s own words and actions,
how
. We regard it as a primary use of the audit trail, not a side effect.
8.7 Limitations
We read this single-period campaign through the standard threats-to-validity frame: what the design can identify, what its constructs assume, and the conditions under which its conclusions hold.
•
What returns can identify.
By construction the returns axis is underpowered to attribute module value at this scale: every single-module
and
cluster knockout lands inside the ±0.42 repeated-run band (§7.7), so no module-importance claim can rest on returns — we read the flat ΔSharpe as a statement about the metric, not the modules, and confine module value to the capability axes (where, e.g., analysis-blind drives Acuity to 0.02). Two related scope conditions: the HIGH cost tier is mis-calibrated (~527 bps/round; LOW retained), and Acuity and Discipline are mode-confounded, so they are read only within mode.
•
Construct validity of the axes.
The five axes operationalize latent capabilities, and the v2 hardening of Discipline and Composure shows the read

is sensitive to how those constructs are defined. We argue construct validity empirically rather than assume it — rank-stability across the revision (τ = 0.78, ρ = 0.90), near-orthogonality (mean |r| = 0.28), and robustness to leave-one-out and judge-reweighting (Appendix F) — and note candidly that the revision overturned one prior sub-finding (memory “breaking” Composure was an artifact of the earlier construction).
•
Judge independence.
A held-out, non-cohort judge mitigates but cannot fully eliminate LLM-as-judge self-preference. A second, different-family judge corroborates the headline gap (GLM-5.2, ρ = 0.65; +0.28/+0.34 vs +0.33/+0.34), yet minimax-m3 also sits in the live cohort, so its own live rounds are self-adjudicated — it rates itself among the lowest (no inflation), but a fully clean design would draw the judge from outside both panels (Appendix C, Table C1).
•
External validity: one regime, modeled backtest costs.
The window is a single, predominantly rising market (SPY +27%, max DD −8.9%, VIX median 17), and backtest costs are modeled rather than executed (the live track supplies real fills on the daily deltas). This bounds every returns-based statement to one regime, and the bound cuts both ways: the benign window
masks
the modules whose value concentrates at turning points and
flatters
high-exposure risk-taking while penalizing the prudent de-risking the agents exhibit (§7.7). The capability and risk-adjusted axes are comparatively regime-robust, but absol

ute-return conclusions should be read as regime-conditional.
•
Live evidence: consistency, not transfer.
The live track shows the instrument measures the same decision property on genuinely unseen data, not that any specific model generalizes — the live and backtest cohorts are different model versions, and live returns are indicative-only by design (daily flatten; overnight exposure is virtual and modeled). The short horizon keeps Acuity and Composure the shortest-baseline axes; tellingly, the second live week overturned two week-1 reads and converted an apparent day-2 hold “collapse” into an intermittent, recoverable rate (§7.11) — itself a caution against over-reading any short live record, ours included.
None of these undercut the contribution — the instrument and its read-out — but all bound what the
numbers
may claim.

The instrument is built to be populated over time; the priorities are the dimensions along which this paper’s reading is thinnest, not a longer list of run parameters.

Scale and statistical power.

•
