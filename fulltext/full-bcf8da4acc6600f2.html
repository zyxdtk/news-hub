摘要
人类对视觉相似性的判断依赖于上下文。例如，两幅图像可能在形状上相似，但在颜色上却截然不同。然而，现有的感知相似性度量将这些细微差别折叠为单个标量值，没有提供基于特定方面进行条件控制的机制。为了弥合这一差距，我们引入了一个关于图像三元组的人类相似性判断的大规模数据集，其中每个三元组都在多个自由形式的语义相似性方面进行了标注。对广泛的前沿视觉-语言模型（VLM）进行基准测试表明，与人类标注者的共识相比，存在相当大的性能差距。利用我们的数据，我们微调了一个VLM，以生成我们的文本提示图像感知相似性（TPIPS）度量，该度量根据指定的文本提示捕捉视觉相似性的多重含义。我们证明，TPIPS与人类感知更紧密地对齐，并且在训练分布之外能够可靠地泛化。最后，我们展示了TPIPS在文本引导检索、组合搜索和生成模型的细粒度评估中解锁了新能力。我们的代码、数据和训练好的模型位于https://peterwang512.github.io/TPIPS。

2 Related Works
Perceptual similarity and preferences.Comparing two images is a fundamental problem in vision,
with early approaches relying on hand-engineered, patch-based metrics [1, 6, 7]. With advances in
deep image recognition [18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23], learned deep features have been found to provide
a more useful similarity signal than hand-engineered descriptors [ 2, 24, 25, 26, 27]. LPIPS [ 2],
PieAPP [28], and DISTS [29] directly optimize deep features to align better with human judgments on
low-level distortions. DreamSim [3] learns human perceptual judgments on synthesized images with
high-level variations. Muttenthaler et al. [30] study perceptual judgments across object categories
2
with THINGS [ 11]. Other works improve metric robustness to spatial shifts [ 31], adversarial
perturbations [32], and arbitrary transformations [33]. Alternative metrics isolate specific attributes
like style [34, 35] or relationships between visual elements [ 36]. While these methods predict a
single similarity score, similarity is inherently multi-f

aceted [4]. We address this by introducing a
benchmark and model for human similarity judgements conditioned on various visual aspects. A
separate but adjacent line of work uses pairwise human comparisons over generated or edited images
to learn a scalar quality score over a single image [37, 38, 39, 40]. In contrast, we learn from triplet
judgments to model pairwise similarity conditioned on visual aspects.
Conditional representation.Various research has studied learning representations of an input
conditioned on different aspects. A line of work explores learning conditional representation given
an explicit aspect, but assumes a fixed, finite vocabulary of conditions [41, 42, 43, 44, 45]. More
recent work generalizes the condition input to free-form text, including training-free methods via
vision-language models [46, 47, 48] and training-based methods that curate conditional supervision.
GeneCIS [8] and FocalLens [ 9] mine attribute-difference triplets from existing vision-language
datasets, while Omni-Attribute [ 10] bootstraps supervision from VLM-generated labels and TF-
QARE [48] prompts for an image’s object, background, or style and averages the embedding of the
resulting description. A parallel line of work studies context-conditional categorization [49, 50], and
composed image retrieval [51, 52, 53, 54] takes image-text inputs but uses the text to specify how

3.1 Odd-one-out dataset

adopt theodd-one-outtask [ 11], and extend it to perceptual judgements conditioned on various visual
aspects. That is, given a triplet, X={x 1,x 2,x 3}, we ask annotators to the pick one that looks most
different under the text guidance c, recording it the result as y. Next, we describe our procedure for

3.2 Out-of-distribution 2AFC dataset
To test generalization to different image variations and content, we curate an additionalout-of-
distribution evaluationset from the outputs of other computer vision algorithms. This choice is
motivated by a practical use case of our framework—evaluating and comparing vision systems—and
by the fact that algorithm outputs provide structured, perceptually meaningful variations.
Unlike our main dataset, this setting is inherentlyreference-based: each query comes with a reference
image (either the algorithm input or the ground-truth target) and multiple candidate outputs. We
therefore adopt a 2-alternative-forced-choice (2AFC) protocol: given an aspect c, annotators are
shown the reference xref and two randomly sampled outputs (x0,x 1), and select which output is
more similar to the reference under c, with multiple votes across annotators averaged as y∈[0,1] .
Similar to the odd-one-out dataset, we collect votes across 5 annotators and remove those with ≥3
“can’t tell” votes. We also remove datapoints that have a tie, as they are not useful for evaluation.
This produces an evaluation dataset Dext ={(x ref,x 0,x 1,c, y)} . The external algorithms and

6 Discussion, Broader Impacts, and Limitations
Beyond using our metric as a human-grained, aspect-conditioned judge, it can serve as a feedback
signal to improve them, as recent work has leveraged VLM feedback to train image editing [74, 75]
and generation [37, 76] models. Our method can also aid in interpretability systems that automatically
discover and characterize neural features [77, 78]. Our metric offers human-aligned measurements
to assess which fine-grained visual properties the discovered features encode. As generative and
perceptual vision systems see broader deployment, interpretability becomes essential for public
oversight, accountability, and informed regulation.

A Dataset collection and curation
We extend Section 3 and provide the full curation pipeline behind the synthetic odd-one-out dataset
used for training and in-distribution evaluation (Section A.1), and the vision-algorithm 2AFC set
used for out-of-distribution evaluation (Section A.2). Throughout, we use the termaspectfor the text
conditionc(e.g.,lighting,color tone,scene geometry).
Annotation crowdsourcing.We collect annotations on Amazon Mechanical Turk and Prolific.
Reward per task is set to target an effective hourly rate of $10/hour. Sentinel checks are applied to
every session. A sentinel triplet is constructed by duplicating one image from a real triplet, making
the correct odd-one-out visually unambiguous. Sessions that fail the sentinels are returned, and the
corresponding responses are excluded.
Per-aspect “can’t tell” filter.To handle the case where the visual aspect condition is ambiguous,
our annotation interface includes a “can’t tell” option in addition to the image choices. We collect
5 annotations per triplet, discard the “can’t tell” vote, and only train and test on the image votes.
To ensure sufficient votes for all visual aspects in the test set, we discard any aspect if 3 or more
annotators mark “can’t tell” in the test set. The filter is applied independently per aspect. A triplet
may keep some aspects and lose others, and we drop the triplet only when no aspe

ct remains.

A.2 2AFC out-of-distribution evaluation set
The 2AFC set is curated from outputs of real vision algorithms across four sources: image editing,
image compositing, novel view synthesis (NVS), and single-image 3D. Each source contributes
a reference image xref and outputs from multiple algorithms; a triplet pairs xref with two outputs
fromdifferentalgorithms, and the annotator picks the output more similar to xref for each aspect.
Algorithms and aspect lists per source are summarized in Table 2.
Image editing.Sources are drawn from GEditBench v2 [ 81], which includes 16 methods. We
keep the 7 methods: BAGEL [ 82], FLUX2-dev [83], LongCat-Image-Edit [84], OmniGen2 [85],
Qwen-Image-Edit [86], Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 [86], Step1X-Edit v1.2 [87]). The rest are dropped
either because they crop or resize the input on at least some samples or because they are near-
duplicate intermediate releases of one of the kept models. We further discard four edit categories
from GEditBench v2 (chart editing, in-image text translation, text editing, and line-to-image) since
they contains visual content out of our interest

（例如，文本渲染、图表、线条草图）。对于每个样本，GPT-5.2 会指出编辑

raining views and render the same held-out poses; the reference image is the held-out
ground-truth photograph at the test pose.
Single-image 3D.We use inputs from 3D Arena and 4 methods: TRELLIS.2 [ 96], InstantMesh [97],
Hunyuan3D-2.1 [98], and Step1X-3D [ 99]. Each method’s mesh is re-rendered in Blender at 4
spaced-out azimuth angles per object; the reference image is the source photograph. Since there is no
consistent canonical view for each method, camera poses from each method are randomly different
from the reference. Hence, we also annotate and evaluate camera pose similarity in this setting.
Annotation interface.Each triplet is presented as [xcand A |x ref |x cand B], with the reference in the
center. Per triplet, annotators are shown the aspect list and select, for each aspect, which candidate
is more similar to the reference, or “can’t tell” (Figure 10). Each (triplet, aspect) is annotated by 5
workers, and we apply the “can’t-tell” filter as before. Following Section 3.2, we additionally drop
aspects whose post-filter vote is exactly tied. Unlike the odd-one-out evaluation, the 2AFC evaluation
does not include an “overall” aspect: every comparison is explicitly conditioned on a visual aspect.

Triplet oracle.Section 5 reports evaluation on pairwise metrics. Our goal is to create an algorithm
that can judge pairwise distance, using the odd-one-out triplet task as a pretext training signal. Here,
though the odd-one-out task is not the primary task, as an advantaged “oracle”, we provide all 3
images to the VLM, and see how closely our pairwise algorithm (which only sees 2 images at a time)
compares. To do so, we ask the model: “Which image is the most different among the three in terms
of ?”, same as what the human annotators answered. This makes even untrained VLMs
strong baselines under the triplet prompt. While Gemini-3.1-Pro reaches 62.0% on odd-one-out and
GPT-5.4 reaches 77.9% total on 2AFC, our early fusion method still outperforms even without the
structural advantage.
In addition, we verify that finetuning a generative VLM to solve the odd-one-out task directly with
the triplet yields the strongest result (see Ours-Triplet). However, such a model produces only a
triplet decision and not a pairwise score, and therefore does not transfer to 2AFC evaluation and
retrieval. The pairwise design is what makes the metric usable across all of these settings.

C.3 Baselines
We provide more

details of the baselines reported in Section 5. We organize baselines by how the two
images interact, plus a separate triplet family that consumes three concatenated images at once. All
baselines use greedy decoding (temperature 0). To avoid position bias of VLMs [101, 102, 103], for
triplet and pairwise-score baselines we apply permutation averaging—6 orderings for odd-one-out,
2 for 2AFC—and map results back to the original positions. Embedding baselines do not need
permutation averaging because cosine similarity is symmetric.

Triplet baselines.The three input images are concatenated horizontally and the model is asked which
side is the odd one out. We use the prompt:
“I provide three horizontally concatenated images (left, middle, right). Which image is the
most different among the three in terms of ⟨c⟩ ? You need to choose: left, middle, right.
Answer:”
For 2AFC the three positions become reference (center) and two candidates (left, right):
“I provide three horizontally concatenated images. The center image is the reference. Which
candidate (left or right) is more similar to the reference in terms of ⟨c⟩ ? Answer with one
word: left or right. Answer:”
When the aspect condition is “overall”, we remove “in terms of⟨c⟩” in these prompts.
For open-weight models, we read logits at the answer position for the three (resp. two) target tokens
and softmax. Models include InternVL3.5 [71], Qwen3-VL [14], and LLaV A-OneVision [63]. API
models (GPT-5.4 [12], Gemini 3.1 Pro [13])

use text parsing.

pair of images is rated for similarity on an integer scale of 0 (completely different) to 10 (identical).
The prompt is:
“I show you two images placed side by side (left and right). Rate how similar these two
images are in terms of ⟨c⟩ on a scale of 0 to 10, where 0 means completely different and 10
means identical. Answer with a single number from 0 to 10. Answer:”
When the aspect is “overall”, we swap out “in terms of⟨c⟩ ” to “overall” in the prompt. We convert
the response to a soft score either as E[d] = P10
d=0 d·P(d) over digit-token logprobs. For API
calls where we can only obtain the generated text, each text response is converted to a one-hot delta

images implies the third is the odd one out); for 2AFC we softmax the two reference–candidate
